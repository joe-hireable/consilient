## D1 — Plugin or standalone?

**Best:** T — it preserves a standalone, host-neutral core while making plugin adoption contingent on a registered breakage test.  
**Worst:** V — plugin-first relies on unverified audience-to-volume benefits and accepts avoidable host-roadmap risk.  
**Gap:** marginal  
**What the best has that the others lack:** A standalone default combined with an explicit pass/fail test before plugin commitment.

## D2 — What is v0’s success condition?

**Best:** M — it requires contrasting repositories, uncertainty bounds, audited proxy labels and prospective treatment of unavailable correlation data without claiming premature stranger value.  
**Worst:** V — it converts unaudited historical proxies and a fragile threshold into an actionable safe/unsafe verdict, risking false-safe routing.  
**Gap:** material  
**What the best has that the others lack:** A measured label-error rate and prospective treatment of ρ rather than treating mined history as ground truth.

## D3 — Does the Inquiry tier belong in v0?

**Best:** T — it makes deferral falsifiable at negligible cost by logging the decision points needed to design the gates from measured need.  
**Worst:** V — its revisit rule depends partly on an unrelated β bound, while its “weekend plugin” and “quadrupling” claims are unearned.  
**Gap:** marginal  
**What the best has that the others lack:** An explicit one-line, no-gates observation mechanism for Inquiry’s own trigger rate.

## D4 — What is in v0, honestly?

**Best:** M — it keeps v0 on authorised measurement, includes a contrast repository, retains externally evidenced budget primitives and records the risk of shipping a meter without an actuator.  
**Worst:** V — it includes a cascade despite acknowledging that uncalibrated routing is broken, omits the contrast safeguard and withholds budgets despite stronger external evidence.  
**Gap:** material  
**What the best has that the others lack:** Contrast-repository measurement and evidence-backed budgets without admitting cascade routing into v0.

## D5 — Does the local model library belong in v0?

**Best:** T — one OpenAI-compatible endpoint preserves both local and hosted cheap tiers without adding discovery, hardware gating or integration code.  
**Worst:** M — it treats shared training data as proof of high outcome correlation without measurement and records no substantive dissent.  
**Gap:** marginal  
**What the best has that the others lack:** A concrete zero-integration route to local models in v0 while avoiding a model-library feature.

## D6 — Executable-model CI ratchet: keep or drop?

**Best:** V — it trials the narrowest useful mechanism on models that already exist, with no framework and the three-month experiment as an explicit kill switch.  
**Worst:** M — it leaves a declared invariant unenforced after the precise sign-flip failure has already occurred, repeating the repository’s documented chokepoint failure mode.  
**Gap:** material  
**What the best has that the others lack:** Existing-model-only scope combined with a pre-specified empirical kill switch for maintenance cost.
