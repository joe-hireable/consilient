# Commercial model — the downside critic, 23 August 2026

Kept whole and unedited. Three findings outrank the strategy document it reviews.

**1. The risk is already live, unpaid, and nothing in the strategy addresses it.** An agent running
on a user's machine with the user's own API keys can spend that user's money tonight. Consumer
Rights Act 2015 s.49 does not care that nothing was charged for the software. The entire
commercial question is scaffolding around an exposure that exists today, and the one control that
matters — **a hard client-side spend ceiling checked before any provisioning call** — is roughly a
day of work that no research arm put first.

**2. The £25 step change.** Gratuitous MIT software leaves the author outside trader status. The
first affiliate commission plausibly makes him a trader under CRA 2015 s.2, and consumer-law
duties then attach to the whole product, for every user, permanently. Twenty-five pounds buys
unlimited consumer-law surface. That is the decision, and no arm framed it as one.

**3. Six agents produced six different valuations for the same checkable fact.** Asked to verify
one number, they returned $500m, $1.3bn, $7bn, $7.5bn, $8bn, and one honest failure — each stated
confidently, while agreeing with each other on everything unfalsifiable. This is the repository's
own echo thesis demonstrated on itself: **agreement among agents sharing a base model is style,
not evidence.** Discount the consensus in the strategy document accordingly.

**Six of them "corrected" his OpenRouter figure and gave six different numbers** — $500m, $1.3bn, $7bn, $7.5bn, $8bn, and one that couldn't verify any. Each stated its own confidently. When eight agents sharing a base model disagree on a single checkable fact while agreeing on everything else, the agreement is style, not evidence. Discount the whole consensus by that much.

## 1. Trace the money — where "never" is false

- **Referral.** Affiliate networks pay net-30/60 with clawback on refund and churn. A clawback on a paid-out commission creates a **negative balance he owes the network**. Small, real, and it is the exact "never touches money" claim breaking.
- **BYOK.** Correct that he holds no funds. Irrelevant to liability. He *causes* spend on someone else's card. Custody and causation are different questions and seven of eight collapsed them.
- **His own infra.** The one flow with certain, unrecoverable cash outflow, scaling with adoption and not with revenue. Every brief filed it under "residual, non-financial: his time". It is a monthly invoice.
- **Merchant of record.** Paddle §8/§10.4 set-off and six-month retention were cited by two agents and then ignored in their own recommendations. An MoR does not remove him from the money; it moves him behind it.

## 2. Most probable year-one loss

Not a breach. Not a chargeback — he will have no payment stack. It is:

- **The Google OAuth restricted-scope security assessment.** Five briefs recommended the inbox-mining subscription dashboard as the "zero-risk" replacement product. Reading Gmail is a restricted scope: mandatory annual third-party CASA assessment, four figures, payable *before launch*, by a sole trader with no revenue. One agent mentioned it in a residual clause without a number. It is the largest certain cash line in the entire strategy and it appears in no Tier 1.
- **Second: his hosting and inference bill** for a free product that gets adopted. Adoption is the stated goal and the stated cost driver simultaneously. Nobody modelled it.

## 3. Regulated activity dressed as a product decision

- The **read-only dashboard** — the thing they all retreated to as safe. UK GDPR Art 35 DPIA (mailbox mining at scale hits ICO high-risk criteria), Art 32, 72-hour notification, ICO registration, plus the CASA audit above. Three agents recommended it in the same breath as "zero regulatory surface". It is the most regulated thing left standing.
- Worse: **it cannot be built for his users anyway.** One brief established Anthropic's Admin API is "unavailable for individual accounts" and Stripe portal sessions are merchant-only. The consumer-facing spend dashboard has no data source. Five agents recommended a product whose enabling API a sixth had already proven does not exist, and none noticed.

## 4. Zero-knowledge

- Honest work **against** the vault — correct, unanimous, keep it.
- Decorative everywhere else. LastPass is a story about a company owned by GoTo with a security team. The argument that actually applies to him is one sentence and nobody led with it: **he cannot patch on a schedule.** He gets ill, users stay exposed, and there is no second person. That kills the vault without a single ICO citation, and it kills the OAuth token store too — which they all waved through.

## 5. Ads vs neutrality

- No reconciliation, and all eight said drop ads. Fine — he'd already decided that in capitals; they billed him for agreeing.
- **The performance is what came next.** Six of them then reinstated commercial influence as "flat-rate, uniform, disclosed affiliate revenue". Taking money that varies with which tool a user adopts *is* pushing tools for commercial gain. A fixed price on the influence is still a price. Either the affiliate revenue goes, or the principle does. Nobody said that plainly, because saying it leaves the answer as "make no money at all", which the brief implicitly forbade them from concluding.

## 6. What the shared brief hid

- **He is holding the risk today, unpaid.** An agent that runs on the user's machine with the user's keys can burn £2,000 of their AWS credit tonight. CRA 2015 s49 does not care that he charged nothing. The whole strategy is scaffolding to avoid a risk he already has, and the one control that matters — a hard client-side spend ceiling before any provisioning call — is a day's work that no brief put first.
- **The step change nobody priced.** MIT gratuitous software → he is not a trader. **First £25 affiliate cheque → he is a trader**, and CRA, DMCC Sch 20 and consumer cancellation rights attach to the whole product, for every user, forever after. £25 buys unlimited consumer-law exposure. That is the actual decision in front of him, and it was never framed as one.
- All eight answered "how should this be monetised". None asked whether it should be. GitHub Sponsors at 0%, no trader status, no commission, no disclosure duty, no CASA audit. That was available on page one.
